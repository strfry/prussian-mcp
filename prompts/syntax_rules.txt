# Prussian Syntax Rules

All morphological rules (inflectional paradigms, derivational suffixes, declension
tables, conjugation endings) are excluded — these are covered by the dictionary.
Only the syntactic, prosodic, and combinatorial rules that cannot be inferred from
dictionary lookups alone are listed below.

Sources (attribution only, not bundled):
  [N] = Nērtiks grammar (Polish)
  [P] = Palmaitis, "Insā Prūsiska Grammatiki"

Sembian dialect, Palmaitis/Klussis system.


§1 — Accent Loss with Monosyllabic Prepositions                                   [N] [P]

Monosyllabic prepositions with a long vowel (ēn, pēr, prēi, nō) lose vowel length
when preceding a polysyllabic word. Before monosyllabic words, the length is retained
and the preposition is typically fused with the word as a compound.

  ēn + buttan   → en buttan     (into the house)
  pēr + araīkan → per araīkan   (abroad)
  prēi + mērgan → prei mērgan  (at the girl)
  nō  + sallin  → na sallin    (on the island)

But: ēnkaps, ēnwangan, nōpalis (before monosyllables the long vowel is kept).


§2 — Nominative Subject and Predicate Nominal                                      [N] [P]

The subject is always in the nominative. With būtwei (to be) and pastātwei (to become),
the predicate nominal also stands in the nominative — not in the instrumental (unlike
Polish or Russian).

  As asma kunnegs.  — I am a king.


§3 — Genitive Pre-Position                                                         [N] [P]

The genitive attribute normally stands BEFORE the noun it modifies. When both a genitive
attribute and an adjective modify the same noun, the adjective's agreement (gender,
number) disambiguates which noun is being described:

  wūras anzanas saknis  — the root of the old oak
      (wūras = gen.sg.masc, agrees with anzanas → "old oak")
  wurā anzanas saknis   — the old root of the oak
      (wurā = nom.sg.fem, agrees with saknis → "old root")


§4 — Plural Genitive Disambiguation with stēisan                                   [N] [P]

The plural genitive ending is identical to the singular accusative ending. To avoid
ambiguity, the plural genitive uses the demonstrative stēisan + accusative plural.
The genitive attribute then follows the head noun:

  saknis stēisan wūran anzanan  — the roots of the old oaks

For a singular genitive that must be moved after its head noun, stesse (masc./neut.)
or stesses (fem.) + accusative singular is used:

  anzanas saknis  → saknis stesse anzanan       (the root of the oak)
  līpas saknis    → saknis stesses līpan         (the root of the linden)

Other determiners with distinct gen.pl and acc.sg forms (wisēisan = all, kitēisan =
other) can substitute for stēisan.


§5 — Dative Usage                                                                  [N] [P]

The dative marks:
  (a) indirect object:
      As tenginna pēismenin tebbei. — I sent you a letter.
      Also covers the semantic range of "to" (Polish "do"):
      Tāns ast mennei peisāwuns. — He has written to me.

  (b) with ezze (from):
      Tenā pagāuwusi stan ezze ti. — She received it from you.

  (c) with pa (under, static):
      Kurmjai buwinna pa zemmei. — Moles live under the earth.

  (d) with ēn (in, static — where?):
      As wīrst būwuns en buttu. — I will be in the house.


§6 — Accusative Usage                                                              [N] [P]

The accusative marks:
  (a) direct object of transitive verbs (infinitive -tun):
      As peisāwuns pēismenin. — I wrote a letter.
      Intransitive verbs (-twei) govern genitive instead.

  (b) with these prepositions:
      sēn  (with)               sēn tin — with you
      prēi (at, by)             prei mērgan — at the girl
      pēr  (for, through)       per sunin — for the dog
      ezze (about)              ezze min — about me
      ēr   (until, temporal)    ēr wisasīdin — until Tuesday
      nō/na (on)                na sallin — on the island
      pa   (after, according to) pa brōkastan — after breakfast
      pas  (following)          pas sunnin — following the dog

  (c) ēn + accusative (dynamic, direction — where to?):
      As wīrst gūbuns en buttan. — I will go into the house.
      (ēn + dative = static; ēn + accusative = dynamic; see §7)


§7 — ēn + Case: Static vs. Dynamic                                                 [N] [P]

  ēn + Dative    = static location (where?):      en buttu   (in the house)
  ēn + Accusative = dynamic direction (where to?): en buttan  (into the house)

The vowel-shortening rule (§1) applies before disyllabic nouns regardless of case.


§8 — Instrumental Replacement                                                      [N] [P]

Prussian has no instrumental case. Its functions are covered by:
  sēn + accusative (instrument, means):
      As pūwa sen laīwan. — I travelled by ship.
  pēr + accusative (predicate, role):
      Tū būlai per kunnegan. — You would be a king.


§9 — Predicative Adjective (Neuter)                                                [N] [P]

An adjective used predicatively with būtwei or pastātwei is always neuter, regardless
of the subject's gender or number:

  Tāns ast labban.   — He is good.
  Tenā ast labban.   — She is good.
  Tenēi ast labban.  — They (masc.) are good.
  Tennas ast labban. — They (fem.) are good.


§10 — Pronominal Adjective ("the good one")                                        [N] [P]

When context fully identifies a referent through the adjective alone ("the first one",
"the good one"), the pronominal form of the adjective (marked pnl, -isis forms) is
used:

  Bēi dwāi brātei — labs be wārgs.
  Labasis bēi aūktan, adder wārgasis zemman.
  There were two brothers — good and bad.
  The good one was tall, and the bad one was short.


§11 — Future Tense                                                                 [N] [P]

Formed with the auxiliary wīrstwei (conjugated in the present) + active past participle.
The participle agrees with the subject in gender and number:

  As wīrst būwuns (m) / būwusi (f).         — I will be.
  Mes wīrstmai gūbusis (m) / gūbušas (f).   — We will go.

A short neuter form may replace the gender-agreeing participle:

  As wīrst būwus. / Mes wīrstmai gūbus.


§12 — Perfect Tense                                                                [N] [P]

Formed with būtwei (conjugated in the present) + active past participle, gender-
agreeing with the subject. Describes a present state resulting from a past action
(completion, present relevance):

  As asma segīwuns stan. — I have done that. (m)
  Jāu as asma pergūbusi. — I have already arrived. (f)


§13 — Pluperfect Tense                                                             [N] [P]

Same construction as the perfect, but būtwei is in the preterite (bēi, bēimai, bēitei).
Used in reported speech and to emphasize anteriority:

  Bītai as jāu bēi tenginnuns pēismenin. — Yesterday I had already sent the letter.


§14 — Passive Voice                                                                [N] [P]

Only transitive verbs (-tun) form a passive. Uses the passive past participle (-ts)
with būtwei or pastātwei. With pastātwei, the participle is always neuter (cf. §9):

  Lūgis ast jāu segītan.   — The cake is already made.
  Kaūlai pastāi mestan.    — The dice are cast.


§15 — Impersonal Construction with di                                              [N] [P]

The particle di replaces the subject to form impersonal constructions:

  tērpaui di pagaptins.   — One uses tools.
  būlai di labban.        — It would be good.
  wīrst di skaitāwus.     — One will read.

When a participle is used, it must be neuter:

  ast di aupallus.        — It has been found.

Some verbs have a special preterite form for use with di (the second pt form in the
dictionary):
  immē di                 — one took (not: imme di)


§16 — Conjunctive (Subjunctive)                                                    [N] [P]

Formed from the infinitive stem + -lai (sg, 3p), -limai (1pl), -litei (2pl). Used as
an irrealis (conditional) and in kāi-complement clauses:

  As segīlai.                           — I would do.
  As madli, kāi tū segīlai stan.        — I ask that you do it.


§17 — Optative                                                                     [N] [P]

3rd person: infinitive stem + -sei ("let him / may he"):
  segīsei   — let him do
  būsei     — let it be

1st person plural optative/imperative = 1pl indicative:
  immimai!  — let us take! (= we take)

For būtwei: sēisei (concrete subject) vs. būsei (impersonal): labbai, būsei di tīt.


§18 — Modus Relativus (Relative / Hearsay Mood)                                     [P]

Marks reported or second-hand information. Formed with -ts on the 3rd person:

  Present:   ast → astits; wedda → weddats; segēi → segēits
  Preterite: verbs in -a → -āts (treppa → treppāts)
             verbs in -i → -ēts (weddi → weddēts)

  Tena gērdaui, geīts kaštūjats jāu dwigubbai. — She says it reportedly already
  costs twice as much.


§19 — Present Participle as Adverbial (Gerund)                                     [N] [P]

The present active participle (-nts) used as an adverbial (converb) by changing its
ending to -intei, -antei, or -āntei, derived from the 3ps stem:

  3ps -a  → -ants/-ānts → -antei/-āntei
  3ps -e  → -ints/-īnts → -intei/-īntei
  3ps -ai → -ānts       → -āntei
  3ps -ei → -īnts       → -īntei

  Jāntei as widāi azzarans. — Riding, I saw the lakes.


§20 — Adverb Formation and Comparison                                              [N] [P]

Adverbs from adjectives: -as/-s → -ai (labs → labbai), -is → -ei, -us → -u.

Comparative adverb: from comparative adjective with -ais/-eis/-uis:
  spārtai → spārtais (more strongly)

Superlative adverb: prefix uka- + comparative adverb:
  spārtais → ukaspārtais (most strongly)

Irregular: tūlin (much) → tūls (more) → ukatūls (most).
           labbai (well) → walnai/walnan (better) → ukawalnai/ukawalnan (best).


§21 — Numerals 1–9                                                                  [N]

Decline like adjectives. Counted noun is plural from 2 onward. Numbers 2 and 3 are
gender-neutral: 2 is always masc./neuter form, 3 is always feminine form.

  dwāi berzis  — two birches


§22 — Numerals 10+ (Substantival)                                                   [N]

Tens, hundreds, thousands are formally nouns (for tens: -ts; for hundreds: -a) and
govern the genitive plural of the counted noun. The counting noun declines; the
counted noun stays in the genitive. No stēisan needed:

  dwiddesimts sunnin   — twenty dogs (lit. "a twentyness of dogs")
  sīmtan wārnin        — a hundred crows (lit. "a hundredness of crows")


§23 — Numerals 10+ (Adjectival, Alternative)                                        [N]

Without the substantival suffixes -s/-a (bare -t), numerals are indeclinable and the
counted noun carries the case:

  dwiddesimt sunnei / sunnin / sunnemans / sunnins


§24 — Quantity Adverbs as Numerals                                                  [N]

Indeclinable; the counted noun retains its full case inflection:

  tūlin anzanai / anzanan / anzanamans / anzanans  — many oaks


§25 — Complex Numbers                                                               [N]

All component numerals have the same form; only the last determines the noun's
declension:

  sīmtan pēnkdesimt trīs anzanai   — 153 oaks (nom.)
  sīmtan pēnkdesimt trijjan anzanan — 153 oaks (gen.)


§26 — Collective Numerals (-erei)                                                   [N]

Used for pluralia tantum. Answer the question kellinsun? ("in how many?"):

  newīnerei dwarris — nine (sets of) gates


§27 — Fractions                                                                    [N]

Cardinal + ordinal (neuter) + measured noun in genitive. No stēisan needed:

  aīnan pēnktan zukan  — one fifth of a fish
  dwāi penktāi zukan   — two fifths of a fish


§28 — Clock Time (Kerdā)                                                            [P]

Minutes + pa + ordinal (past); minutes + pirzdau + ordinal (before/till).
"Half" = pussi + genitive of the ordinal:

  Pussi Asmas                      — half past seven
  Punkt uššas                      — six o'clock sharp
  Pēnkis minūtis pa ketturins      — five past four
  Ketwirtadelīks pirzdau aīnadesimt — quarter to eleven
